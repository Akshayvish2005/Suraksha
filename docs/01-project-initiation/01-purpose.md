# 1. Purpose of the Project

## 1.1 Problem statement

In India, schools and colleges are often unprepared for natural disasters such as
earthquakes, floods and fires. Emergency guidelines exist on paper, but there is:

- no structured disaster management education integrated into the curriculum, and
- no digital tools to simulate disaster scenarios or run virtual drills to train
  students and staff on safety protocols.

## 1.2 Why this matters

India is exposed to multiple natural hazards. Large parts of the country fall in
moderate-to-high seismic zones, many regions flood every monsoon, and building fires
are a recurring risk in crowded institutions. Educational institutions are especially
sensitive because they concentrate hundreds or thousands of young people in one place,
often in buildings of varying quality, at predictable times of day.

## 1.3 Gaps this project addresses

| # | Gap today | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guidelines exist only as documents and circulars | People do not read, remember or practise them |
| 2 | Disaster education is not a structured part of the curriculum | Knowledge is fragmented, one-time, and easily forgotten |
| 3 | No tools to simulate emergencies safely | Students never experience decision-making under pressure |
| 4 | Physical mock drills are infrequent, disruptive and often tokenistic | Low participation, no repetition, no learning from mistakes |
| 5 | No way to measure readiness | Institutions cannot tell what their real preparedness level is |
| 6 | Content is rarely localised (language, region, building type) | Low relevance and poor reach |

## 1.4 Purpose statement

> **To build a digital education system that makes disaster preparedness a routine,
> practical and measurable part of learning in Indian schools and colleges — by combining
> curriculum-aligned learning, interactive scenario simulations and virtual drills.**

## 1.5 Who benefits

| Stakeholder | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Students | Learn what to do, and practise doing it, before a real emergency |
| Teachers / faculty | Ready-made teaching content and drill tools; clear roles in an emergency |
| Administrators / principals | Compliance support, drill records and a readiness score |
| Non-teaching and security staff | Role-specific training (wardens, fire marshals, first-aid) |
| Parents and community | Confidence that the institution is prepared |
| Disaster management authorities | A scalable way to reach institutions with standard training |

## 1.6 Alignment with existing frameworks

- **Disaster Management Act, 2005** — establishes the national framework and the role
  of NDMA, state and district authorities.
- **NDMA guidelines on school safety** — call for safety plans, drills and capacity
  building in schools.
- **NEP 2020** — emphasises experiential and skills-based learning, which simulations
  and virtual drills directly support.

The system is intended to *operationalise* these guidelines, not replace them.

## 1.7 Expected long-term outcomes

- Faster, calmer and more correct responses during real emergencies
- Higher awareness and retention of safety knowledge among students and staff
- Institutions that can demonstrate and improve their readiness with data
- A reusable, low-cost model that can be scaled to many institutions
